# Trail Tails

Status: concept proposal
Product: paws.money v5
Format: solo, turn-based exploration puzzle
Business model: free to play; capped rewards in internal PAWS only

## Product promise

Choose one of your pets and guide it through a short, cozy expedition. Read the
trail, spend a small set of abilities carefully, recover the lost keepsake, and
get home safely. A round should take three to five minutes and leave the player
with a story about what their pet did—not merely a score.

The memorable moment is the final choice: take the known safe path home, or use
what you learned about the map to attempt one last optional rescue.

## Why this belongs in paws.money

- The player's owned pet is the hero, not a decorative avatar beside the game.
- Existing hunger, happiness, and health become readable context without being
  an entry fee or something the player can lose by failing.
- Rewards feed the existing PAWS ledger and store loop.
- Future toys, treats, and cosmetics can create new play styles without selling
  power or exposing real-money value to game randomness.
- It expands Paw Match into a recognizable games ecosystem: Paw Match is the
  quick memory game; Trail Tails is the deeper daily adventure.

## Core loop

1. **Choose a pet.** The server verifies ownership and that the pet is alive.
2. **Review the trail.** The player sees a small illustrated map with the start,
   home, nearby terrain, and the expedition objective. Most tiles are hidden.
3. **Take a turn.** Move one tile or spend one of three limited abilities:
   `sniff`, `dash`, or `rest`.
4. **Read the result.** The server reveals terrain, updates energy, and presents
   any encounter as a clear choice rather than an opaque dice roll.
5. **Find the keepsake.** Recover the required objective; optional discoveries
   improve the star rating.
6. **Return home.** Finishing safely awards progress and, while under the daily
   cap, a fixed PAWS reward based on completed objectives.

There is no entry fee, wager, purchasable retry, or loss of PAWS.

## Rules for the first version

### Board

- A compact 5×5 trail generated by the server for each session.
- One start tile, one home tile, one keepsake, one optional rescue, and terrain
  such as meadow, creek, brambles, and lookout.
- The map must always have a solvable route to the keepsake and home.
- Unrevealed tiles never appear in API responses or page source.

### Turns and energy

- A round begins with 12 energy.
- Moving normally costs one energy.
- Difficult terrain can cost one additional energy, but its cost is visible
  before the player commits once that terrain has been scouted.
- Reaching zero energy does not hurt the pet. The expedition ends and may be
  retried for fun with no reward penalty.

### Abilities

- **Sniff:** reveal the terrain of all orthogonally adjacent tiles; two uses.
- **Dash:** cross one already-revealed normal tile without spending energy; one
  use.
- **Rest:** recover two energy on a safe tile; one use.

Abilities are initially species-neutral so every pet is viable. Species traits
can be added later only after the species vocabulary is normalized and each
trait has an equally useful counterpart.

### Scoring

The player earns stars for explicit accomplishments, not client timing:

- 1 star: recover the keepsake and return home.
- 2 stars: also complete the optional rescue.
- 3 stars: complete both goals with at least three energy remaining.

The server derives the star rating from its persisted state. The client never
submits a score, completion flag, elapsed time, or reward amount.

## Pet integration

For the MVP, choosing a pet changes the hero name, portrait treatment, and
story text. A healthy pet receives an encouraging readiness badge, but current
care stats do not change the odds or reward. This avoids turning feeding into a
hidden fee or punishing players for returning after time away.

Completing a trail grants non-financial **bond XP** to that pet. Bond levels can
later unlock titles, map frames, emotes, and trail journals. Bond XP is stored
separately from PAWS and cannot be transferred, sold, or withdrawn.

## Reward economy

- Trail Tails awards internal, non-convertible PAWS only.
- Suggested reward: 5 PAWS for one star, 8 for two, and 10 for three.
- The platform should enforce one shared game-reward budget per account per UTC
  day rather than a separate exploitable cap for every new game.
- After the shared cap is reached, all games remain playable and clearly say
  that the round is for fun.
- Cosmetic and bond rewards may continue after the PAWS cap, subject to their
  own anti-abuse limits.
- USD, deposits, withdrawals, crypto addresses, and provider APIs never enter
  the game service or game request schemas.

Before PAWS is ever made convertible or withdrawable, game rewards must be
disabled or redesigned following legal review and a dedicated abuse analysis.

## Trust boundary and anti-cheat model

The browser is an untrusted renderer and input device. It may submit only the
requested action and a fresh idempotency key.

```text
untrusted browser
    -> authenticated action { sessionId, action, actionId }
    -> ownership check + strict schema
    -> user/session row locks
    -> server validates and applies one legal turn
    -> server derives public view, stars, and capped reward
    -> idempotent double-entry PAWS settlement
```

Required controls:

- Use Node's OS-backed cryptographic RNG when generating a session.
- Generate until the server's solver proves the trail is completable.
- Store private map state only in PostgreSQL; serialize a separate public view.
- Reject unknown request properties, including `reward`, `score`, `currency`,
  `map`, `energy`, `stars`, and `completed`.
- Scope every pet and session query to the authenticated user.
- Lock the user and session rows in a consistent order for every turn.
- Store the exact response for each UUID action key and replay it for retries.
- Derive ledger idempotency from the server-created session ID.
- Rate-limit session creation and actions independently.
- Expire abandoned sessions and allow at most one active Trail Tails session per
  user.
- Log impossible or repeatedly rejected actions as abuse signals without
  revealing private state.

DevTools can alter what the player sees locally, but cannot create a legal move,
change persisted state, mint PAWS, or learn the hidden map.

## Suggested API

```text
POST /api/v1/games/trail-tails
  { petId, actionId }

GET /api/v1/games/trail-tails/:sessionId

POST /api/v1/games/trail-tails/:sessionId/actions
  { action: "move", direction: "north", actionId }
  { action: "sniff", actionId }
  { action: "dash", direction: "east", actionId }
  { action: "rest", actionId }
```

Each response contains only the current public map, pet presentation data,
remaining energy and abilities, legal action hints, objective state, and any
already-settled reward.

## Data model direction

Do not stretch Paw Match's `board`, `matchedPositions`, and `firstPosition`
columns into a polymorphic game engine. Add purpose-built tables:

- `trail_sessions`: owner, pet, status, private map, public discoveries,
  position, energy, ability charges, objectives, stars, reward, timestamps.
- `trail_actions`: session, owner, idempotency key, action, exact response.
- `pet_game_progress`: pet, game type, bond XP, bond level, cosmetics.
- `daily_game_rewards`: user, UTC date, awarded PAWS, rewarded completions.

The shared daily reward record becomes the economic choke point for Paw Match,
Trail Tails, and future games.

## Experience and visual direction

Trail Tails should feel like a pocket storybook: warm paper, inked paths,
friendly terrain shapes, and restrained motion. The main screen is the map,
with the chosen pet always visible at its current tile. Energy and abilities
sit beside the map as physical trail-kit objects rather than dashboard metrics.

Important states must work without color alone. Every terrain type uses a name,
icon, and shape; keyboard and touch controls receive equal support; reduced
motion replaces travel animation with a quick cross-fade.

## MVP boundary

Build only:

- one biome;
- one objective plus one optional rescue;
- the three universal abilities;
- solo play with one owned pet;
- bond XP and the three fixed PAWS reward tiers;
- the shared daily PAWS cap;
- a deterministic solver and server-authoritative action engine;
- responsive, keyboard-accessible map UI;
- security, concurrency, idempotency, and reward-cap tests.

Defer species powers, items, multiple biomes, social leaderboards, tournaments,
pet-versus-pet play, tradable rewards, and real-money features until the core
loop is demonstrably fun and abuse-resistant.

## Success criteria

- A new player understands the objective and makes a first move within 20
  seconds.
- A typical successful round lasts three to five minutes.
- Players can explain why they earned each star.
- Returning players choose different routes rather than memorizing one board.
- The full private map and reward calculation never reach the browser.
- Replays, concurrent actions, forged fields, cross-user IDs, and cap races do
  not produce duplicate state or value.
- The game remains complete and enjoyable after PAWS rewards are exhausted.

## First playtest question

Does the final safe-path-versus-optional-rescue choice create a story worth
retelling, or does it feel like merely optimizing energy? That is the first
thing to prototype before expanding the economy or content.
